Serbia votes out Vučić, Brazil keeps Lula, and a personal cancer vaccine passes a big test.

01

Serbia’s ruling party loses power after 14 years

Aleksandar Vučić, who has led Serbia as prime minister or president since 2014, admitted on Sunday night that his party had lost the parliamentary election to the Student List, a group that grew out of two years of student-led protests against corruption. Early counts by independent monitors give the students about 52% of the vote and a majority in the 250-seat parliament, and turnout of close to 70% was the highest since Yugoslavia broke up.

Sources: CBS News · CBC · RTÉ

02

Lula wins Brazil’s presidency again, and his rival refuses to accept it

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, 80, won Sunday’s runoff, the second-round vote between the top two candidates, beating Flávio Bolsonaro, a senator and ally of Donald Trump, by about 52% to 48% to earn a fourth term. Bolsonaro said he would not recognise the result and would go to court, pointing to a last-minute Supreme Court decision that kept polls open two hours longer.

Sources: NPR · PBS · France 24

03

A cancer vaccine made for each patient keeps melanoma from coming back more often

In a large trial of 1,137 people who had a dangerous skin cancer called melanoma cut out, adding a vaccine built from each person’s own tumour to a standard immune drug lowered the risk of the cancer returning or the patient dying by 42% over about two years. The full results, from Merck and Moderna, were presented at Europe’s biggest cancer conference on Saturday, but it is not yet clear whether the vaccine helps people live longer, and it is not approved anywhere.

Sources: News-Medical · Targeted Oncology

04

Milwaukee comes back again to lead the World Series 2–0

The Milwaukee Brewers fell four runs behind in the first inning on Saturday, then beat the Tampa Bay Rays 5–4 to win the second game of baseball’s best-of-seven championship series. Game 3 is in Tampa Bay tonight.

Sources: ESPN · MLB
