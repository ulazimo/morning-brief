Serbia prepares to pick a president, climate talks fail, and an AI-aided proof stuns mathematicians.

01

Serbia must call a presidential election by Friday

Aleksandar Čupić, the new speaker of parliament who is also standing in as head of state, has until 27 November to set the vote for 20 or 27 December, and the Student List, which won October’s parliamentary election, says it has a candidate but has not named them. Aleksandar Vučić, who cannot run again after two terms, called the vote a farce and said his Serbian Progressive Party may stay out, with the party due to decide on 13 December.

Sources: Nova · Vreme · Glas Slavonije

02

World climate talks end without a deal

The UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, broke up on Sunday with no agreement, after oil-producing countries such as Saudi Arabia got wording about moving away from coal, oil and gas cut from the draft and the European Union refused to accept the weaker text. Every country must agree for a deal to pass, so the 2023 promise to “transition away” from those fuels still stands, but nothing new was added.

Sources: CBS News · NBC News · CBC

03

Trump says he may restart attacks on Iran after New Year

In a Fox News interview on Sunday, President Donald Trump said he is inclined to resume strikes on Iran if there is no agreement by the end of the year, adding that Iran shows no sign of wanting a deal he could accept. Two weeks ago he had called new attacks “highly unlikely” if talks on Iran’s nuclear programme and on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, the sea lane that normally carries about a fifth of the world’s oil, made progress.

Sources: Times of Israel · CNBC

04

A mathematician says AI helped solve maths’ most famous puzzle

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, said on Friday that he used the company’s Claude AI to prove the Riemann hypothesis, a 167-year-old unsolved problem about the pattern of prime numbers (numbers divisible only by 1 and themselves), and posted the proof in Lean, a computer language that checks every logical step. Several mathematicians told reporters they were astonished and expect it may hold up, but it has not yet been independently confirmed and could take years for people to fully understand.

Sources: Nature · Science · Washington Post
