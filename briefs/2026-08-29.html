Mladić Is Dead. The Balkans Are Already Fighting Over His Funeral.

Top Stories This Morning

01

Ratko Mladić dies in UN custody; Serbia announces a state funeral

The Bosnian Serb wartime general, convicted of genocide for the 1995 Srebrenica massacre and the siege of Sarajevo, died Thursday at 84 in the UN detention unit in The Hague, 15 years into a life sentence. Serbia's justice ministry says his body will be flown to Belgrade and buried with full military and state honours, likely at Topčider cemetery.

Why it matters: The decision has split the region along the same lines the war did three decades ago — Srebrenica survivor groups and the UN have condemned any glorification of a convicted genocidaire, while Belgrade's plan lands weeks before Serbia's own snap election.

Sources: CNN, Balkan Insight, RFE/RL

02

Nearly 2,500 still missing after Nepal-China border floods

A glacial-lake outburst triggered flash floods that tore through the Himalayan border region between Nepal and China last week. At least 579 people are confirmed dead and close to 1,900 remain unaccounted for, including hundreds of foreign nationals from more than 30 countries.

Why it matters: It is one of the deadliest disasters in the Himalayas in years, and rescue teams say wrecked infrastructure and power outages are still slowing the search for survivors.

Sources: Bloomberg, Washington Post

03

Nvidia agrees to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion

Nvidia has struck a deal to acquire Hugging Face, the most widely used hub for sharing and downloading open-source AI models, according to multiple reports citing people familiar with the matter. The price is nearly three times the startup's last funding valuation.

Why it matters: It pulls Nvidia deeper into software and cloud services beyond chips, and hands one company unusual influence over the infrastructure much of the open-source AI ecosystem runs on.

Sources: CNBC, Bloomberg

Serbia & the Balkans

04

Snap election set for October, as regulator clears sale of independent media

President Aleksandar Vučić confirmed Serbia's early parliamentary election will fall on 18 or 25 October, capping more than a year of student-led protests. Days earlier, Serbia's competition commission approved the sale of N1, Nova S and other outlets in the Adria News Network to a Luxembourg fund tied to Euronews's owners.

Why it matters: The outlets are among the last carrying reporting critical of Vučić's government; press-freedom groups warn the sale, coming just before the vote, could reshape coverage of the campaign.

Sources: European Western Balkans, Balkan Insight, N1

World

05

Russian strike kills 27 in Kyiv region as drone barrages continue

A missile and drone strike hit warehouses in the Kyiv region this week, killing 27 people and wounding more than 40, part of a renewed wave of attacks on the capital's outskirts. Ukraine says Russia is massing for fresh offensives toward Kyiv and Chernihiv.

Sources: Ukrinform, United24 Media

06

Gaza's US-backed envoy warns ceasefire is at a "point of no return"

The lead US official overseeing the October truce says continued violations by both sides risk collapsing it entirely. Gaza's health ministry says nearly 1,300 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire began, while aid deliveries remain well below agreed levels.

Why it matters: It is the clearest warning yet that the deal underpinning the post-war plan for Gaza is fraying, even as Hamas has agreed in principle to disarm.

Source: Al Jazeera

Business, Economy & Markets

Wall Street closes the week higher on a Nvidia-led tech rally

Technology

08

Anthropic wins first round against the Pentagon over "supply chain risk" label

A federal judge ruled the Defense Department's designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk was unlawful retaliation, ordering it removed. The dispute began after Anthropic refused to lift safety limits that would have let the military use Claude for autonomous weapons or mass surveillance.

Why it matters: It's a rare legal check on the government's use of national-security labels against a company, and a signal on how far AI firms can go in refusing military use cases.

Sources: CNN, TechCrunch

Science

09

NASA's Roman Space Telescope reaches the pad for tomorrow's launch

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope has been enclosed in its SpaceX Falcon Heavy fairing and rolled to the pad at Kennedy Space Center, on track to launch nine months ahead of schedule.

Why it matters: Roman's wide-field infrared camera will survey the sky roughly 100 times faster than Hubble, aimed at mapping dark energy and finding thousands of new exoplanets.

Sources: SpaceNews, NASA
