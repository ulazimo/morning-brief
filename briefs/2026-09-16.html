The Bond Market Votes Before the Fed Does

Top Stories This Morning

01

The 10-year Treasury yield passes 5% for the first time since 2007

The benchmark US 10-year yield rose above 5% on Tuesday, reaching about 5.04% — its highest since July 2007, and up from 4.6% at the end of July. Futures now put the odds of a quarter-point increase at today's Federal Reserve meeting above 90%, which would take the target range to 3.75–4.00%. The driver is energy: WTI is up roughly 15% this month to near $99 and Brent traded around $107.50, a four-month high, after Saudi Arabia shut the East–West pipeline it had been using to bypass the closed Strait of Hormuz. Chair Kevin Warsh said in July he wanted an "unfiltered message from markets"; the market has sent one.

Why it matters: A central bank raising rates into a supply shock cannot touch the cause of the inflation it is responding to — but the 10-year is the price off which mortgages, corporate debt and emerging-market borrowing are set worldwide, and it has just moved to a level today's borrowers have never worked with.

Sources: CNBC · Investing.com · CNN Business · Kiplinger

02

NATO shoots down a drone over Lithuania, and a Russian warship fires at a Danish helicopter

Italian fighter jets on Baltic air-policing duty destroyed a drone over Lithuania shortly after midnight on Tuesday, near the village of Pratkūnai in the Kaišiadorys district, roughly 95km west of Vilnius. Lithuania's military said it was the first time a drone had been shot down in Lithuanian airspace; two senior officials said the device was probably carrying explosives. Officials did not say whether it was Russian or Ukrainian. Separately, Denmark said a Russian frigate fired two flares at one of its air force helicopters in international waters off Gedser on Monday, narrowly missing it. Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen called the incident serious but not surprising, and part of a pattern of escalating Russian hybrid pressure on Europe.

Why it matters: NATO has now fired on an object over its own territory, a day after Secretary-General Mark Rutte warned that strikes near alliance territory would increase its support for Kyiv. Each incident shortens the distance between harassment and an armed incident between nuclear powers.

Sources: NBC News · France 24 · AP via US News

03

Serbia's student movement puts 250 names and 75,000 signatures on the ballot

Thousands marched on Monday evening from the University of Belgrade Rectorate to the Republic Electoral Commission, carrying a replica of the Pobednik monument, and delivered the boxes of signatures registering "Studentska lista — Studenti pobeđuju" for the 25 October snap parliamentary election. The movement published its full 250-candidate slate on studenti.org.rs at the moment of filing. President Aleksandar Vučić's Serbian Progressive Party filed first, on Saturday, with 224,557 signatures; a third anti-government list around Marinika Tepić and Zdravko Ponoš is still gathering.

Why it matters: The protest movement that grew out of the November 2024 Novi Sad station canopy collapse has converted street pressure into a candidate list for the first time. Serbia allocates seats by D'Hondt, which penalises a divided opposition — so whether these two anti-government columns run separately is now the central arithmetic of the election.

Sources: Balkan Insight · ANSA · Euronews

Serbia & the Balkans

04

The Hague prepares for crowds as Kosovo's KLA leadership awaits judgment

Dutch police spent Tuesday preparing for rallies outside the Kosovo Specialist Chambers, where a first-instance verdict against former president Hashim Thaçi, Kadri Veseli, Rexhep Selimi and Jakup Krasniqi is due this morning. Supporters travelled from Kosovo for the pronouncement; there were rallies in Pristina over the weekend. The four are charged with individual and command responsibility for a campaign of persecution at KLA detention sites in Kosovo and northern Albania in 1998–99, including torture, enforced disappearance and 102 murders. Prosecutors requested 45 years each in February; all four deny the charges and their lawyers have asked for acquittal. BIRN describes it as the only senior-leadership war crimes trial running anywhere at present.

Why it matters: The verdict lands five and a half weeks before Serbia's election and will be read in Pristina and Belgrade as a judgment on the war itself rather than on four defendants — which is why both governments have been preparing for the reaction rather than the ruling.

Sources: Kosovo Specialist Chambers · Balkan Insight · RFE/RL · European Western Balkans

World

05

Saudi Arabia strikes back in Yemen after a missile barrage on its southern air base

Saudi forces hit Houthi strongholds in Yemen on Tuesday, a day after the group fired ballistic missiles and drones at King Khalid Air Base near Khamis Mushait and at civilian areas in Abha and Taif. The Saudi-led coalition said 13 civilians were injured and seven homes and two vehicles damaged; the Houthis said they had targeted hangars, radars, runways and ammunition depots, and called it retaliation for earlier Saudi strikes on Yemen. The National reports nearly 94,000 people displaced as the exchange has widened.

Why it matters: The Saudi–Houthi front that a 2022 truce had largely frozen is open again, and it runs directly through the infrastructure the oil market is currently depending on — the Red Sea export terminals and the pipeline that carries crude to them.

Sources: AP via Washington Times · The National · TRT World

06

The US Supreme Court blocks the new mail-ballot rule for November's midterms

A majority of the court refused to let the administration enforce a Postal Service rule on mail voting, leaving a Boston federal judge's injunction in place and finding the government unlikely to succeed on the merits. Issued in late August under a presidential executive order, the rule would have required states to redesign ballot envelopes with an election-mail logo, high-speed scanning capability and a barcode tied to each voter, and to submit voter details to a USPS portal for review before ballots could be sent. Justice Brett Kavanaugh concurred, writing that applying the rule this year would be arbitrary and capricious under the Administrative Procedure Act because election officials do not have time to implement it. Justices Alito and Thomas dissented.

Why it matters: With voting starting in a matter of weeks, the rules for the midterms are now fixed — and the reasoning that fixed them came from a Trump appointee, on timing rather than on the Postal Service's underlying authority, which remains open.

Sources: NPR · Votebeat · NBC News · CNBC

Business, Economy & Markets

07

Tuesday's session: everything sold off at once, ahead of the Fed

Wall Street fell as yields climbed. The S&P 500 lost 0.45% to 7,585.73, the Nasdaq Composite 0.78% to 25,981.57 and the Dow 328 points, or 0.63%, to 52,093.11. Europe was weaker still: the STOXX 600 slipped 0.6% to a three-month low and the DAX fell about 1.1%, with banks down 1.3% and financial services 2.7% — UBS −4.2%, UniCredit −2.3%, Standard Chartered −2.1% — while LVMH and Hermès each shed close to 2.5% after China's retail sales missed forecasts. Brent held near $107.50 and WTI near $99 with the Saudi pipeline still down. Gold fell 1.2% to $4,263 an ounce, a six-week low, as the dollar index rose to 99.57, its highest since 3 September. Bitcoin traded around $76,900, roughly a third below its level a year ago.

Why it matters: Shares, gold and crypto falling together while the dollar and yields rise is not rotation between assets — it is the repricing that happens when the risk-free rate moves. Everything is being discounted against a 5% 10-year.

Sources: CNBC · Reuters via Investing.com · USAGOLD · Fortune

08

China's factories beat forecasts while its consumers and investors do not

August industrial output rose 5.2% year on year, up from 4.5% in July and ahead of the 4.8% expected in a Reuters poll of 42 analysts. Retail sales grew just 0.4%, down from 0.6% and well short of the 0.8% forecast. Fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% over the first eight months of the year, a deeper contraction than the 6.7% decline recorded through July. Urban surveyed unemployment ticked up to 5.3% from 5.2%.

Why it matters: Production is running well ahead of domestic demand, which means the surplus goes abroad — the mechanism behind the trade friction now building with both Brussels and Washington. It is also why European luxury shares fell on Tuesday: Chinese households are the marginal buyer, and they are not buying.

Sources: CNBC · Reuters via Business Recorder · National Bureau of Statistics of China

Science

09

A precise first burn more than doubles the Roman telescope's potential lifetime

NASA said its Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, launched on 30 August aboard a Falcon Heavy, spent only about 9% of its propellant on the first of two mid-course corrections on 31 August. The burn was accurate enough that, combined with other savings during launch and early operations, the agency expects the mission's potential operational lifetime to more than double. The second correction is planned for later this month, and Roman should settle into its halo orbit around the Earth–Sun L2 point in early December, with commissioning continuing in parallel.

Why it matters: Roman is a wide-field survey instrument built to map dark energy and find exoplanets, and survey science compounds with observing time. Propellant, not optics, is what usually ends missions like this one.

Sources: NASA · NASA (first burn) · NASASpaceflight

10

Screwworm turns up in a Texas horse, a first for the US outbreak

The US Department of Agriculture confirmed New World screwworm in a horse in Texas on Tuesday — the first equine case recorded in the country. Two cases are currently active in the state, the other in a dog in Crockett County. The parasite, whose larvae feed on the living tissue of warm-blooded animals, was eradicated from the United States decades ago and had not been detected here since 2017 until a three-week-old calf in Zavala County tested positive on 3 June. USDA is releasing sterile screwworm flies in Texas, the technique that cleared the fly from North America the first time.

Why it matters: Each new host species and each new county makes containment harder, and the sterile-fly barrier that has held the screwworm south of Panama is the only thing standing between the outbreak and the US cattle herd.

Sources: KSAT · USDA APHIS · CDC

11

Dacia asks Brussels to stop moving the rules on small cars

Dacia chief executive Katrin Adt called on EU lawmakers to freeze regulation on small cars, arguing that successive tightening of emissions and safety requirements is the main reason they have become unaffordable, and that Europe has lost roughly three million annual sales since 2020. The Commission's December automotive package already proposes a new M1E sub-category for electric cars up to 4.2 metres long, carrying a ten-year regulatory freeze once a model is certified and 1.3 fleet-CO2 credits for each EU-built car sold, with the aim of bringing prices into the €15,000–€20,000 range. A further announcement on cheaper small EVs is expected in December.

Why it matters: The small affordable car is the segment European manufacturers abandoned first and where Chinese brands are now strongest; the regulatory freeze is the industry's asking price for coming back to it.

Sources: Autocar · Automotive News Europe · ETSC
