Thursday, 26 November 2026

Serbia sets a date to pick a president, the US plans new import taxes, and Nvidia takes its founder back.

01

Serbia calls its presidential election today

Aleksandar Čupić, the speaker of parliament who is standing in as head of state, is due to sign the order at noon, and he has said 27 December is the only possible voting day. Candidates must sign up at least 20 days before the vote; the Student List, which won October’s parliamentary election, has not yet officially named its choice, and Aleksandar Vučić’s Serbian Progressive Party says it will decide on 13 December whether to take part.

Sources: B92 · N1 · Danas

02

The US plans new import taxes on China, the EU and 12 others

On Wednesday the Trump administration proposed new tariffs, which are taxes paid on goods brought into the country, capped at 15%, counting duties already charged, on goods from the European Union, Japan and South Korea, and an extra 20% on Chinese goods, arguing that these countries’ factories make far more than their own buyers want. The plan uses a trade law that courts have upheld before, after the Supreme Court in February threw out Trump’s earlier worldwide tariffs, and officials say it will probably take months to come into force.

Sources: CNBC · RTÉ · Washington Post

03

Ukraine sinks a Russian warship in a Libyan port

Sea drones, small unmanned boats packed with explosives, hit the Russian navy ship Merkury at a naval base in Benghazi, Libya, on Sunday, and satellite pictures show it lying on its side; Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelensky praised the attack as exemplary. Libyan forces in the east say two of their officers died, and Italy told Ukraine it was wrong to carry the war into the Mediterranean.

Sources: CNBC · Naval News · Kyiv Independent · Just Security

04

Nvidia takes back its founder

Nvidia, the company whose chips power most of the world’s artificial intelligence, on Monday reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive 11 days after he stepped down, although its board had said days earlier it would not, and President Trump says he talked it round. The company said US officials judged his return no risk to national security, but its own inquiry into reports that a foreign spy service targeted him is still going on.

Sources: Nvidia · Axios · CNBC

05

Iran says more tankers hit mines near the Gulf

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said three oil tankers struck sea mines on Tuesday and three more ships, including one carrying gas, on Wednesday in the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow sea lane that normally carries about a fifth of the world’s oil. Iran has made several such claims this month to pressure the US, which is blocking its ports, and none has been independently confirmed.

Sources: CNBC · Saudi Gazette · Jerusalem Post
